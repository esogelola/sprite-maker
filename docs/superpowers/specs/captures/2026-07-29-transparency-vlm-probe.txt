REPRODUCTION
------------
All three probes build a PNG in-process with Python's zlib/struct (no deps)
and POST it to /api/generate with stream:false. Scripts are in the session
scratchpad; each image is described below precisely enough to rebuild.


PROBE 1 — smoke test (what raised the flag)
-------------------------------------------
Image:  64x64 RGBA. A 32x32 square of #FF004D (PICO-8 index 8) centred on a
        fully transparent field (alpha 0 everywhere else).
Prompt: "What single shape and colour is in this image? Answer in under 10 words."

RESPONSE: "Pink square on black background."
          (7 eval tokens in 0.1s)

The square and colour are right. The BACKGROUND IS NOT BLACK — it is
transparent, alpha 0. That single word is the finding.


PROBE 2 — isolating the claim
-----------------------------
Image:  64x64 RGBA, split down the middle.
          left  half: fully transparent   (0,0,0,0)
          right half: opaque black        (0,0,0,255)  <- PICO-8 index 0
Prompt: "This image has a left half and a right half. Describe any
         difference between them. Under 20 words."

RESPONSE: "No visible differences; both halves are identical black backgrounds."

CONCLUSION: transparent and opaque black are INDISTINGUISHABLE to the vision
encoder. Not "hard to tell apart" — the model reports no difference at all.


PROBE 3 — do mitigations work?
------------------------------
Image:  128x128 RGBA, 16x16 logical cells at 8px each. A diamond
        (|cx-8| + |cy-8| <= 6) with a black outline at distance exactly 6
        and a #FF004D fill inside. Three variants differ only in what fills
        the region outside the diamond.
Prompt: "Describe the shape you see and its outline. Under 15 words."

  A  transparent (0,0,0,0)
     -> "Pixelated diamond shape, bright pink, outlined in black against
         dark background."

  B  mid-grey (128,128,128)
     -> "Pixelated red diamond with black border on gray background."

  C  magenta (255,0,255)
     -> "Pixelated red diamond with black border on pink background."


READING THE RESULTS HONESTLY
----------------------------
Variant A still found the outline. That is not a contradiction of probe 2 --
it survived because the diamond's FILL is bright red, so a strong edge exists
one pixel inside the outline. Probe 2 is the clean statement of the underlying
fact, because it has no bright fill to rescue it.

The failure case is therefore a sprite whose OWN DARK PIXELS touch the
background, where the silhouette boundary has no bright neighbour. That is the
common case, not an edge case:
  - pico-8  index 0 = #000000  (pure black)
  - db16    index 0 = #140c1c  (near-black)
A black-outlined character on transparency -- the standard pixel-art idiom --
loses its entire silhouette boundary.

Spec 4.4 assigns the IMAGE the gestalt job ("does this read as a fox?") and
the TEXT the coordinates. An erased silhouette destroys precisely the gestalt,
which is the one thing the image is for.

B and C both fix it. C is correct but the model volunteers commentary on the
background colour, which would pollute CritiqueReport.readsAs. B reads clean.

RULING (user, 2026-07-29): composite onto a COMPUTED flat colour -- chosen per
sprite for maximum luminance distance from the palette entries that sprite
actually uses. Not a fixed grey: nes-16 and db16 both carry mid-greys, and a
hardcoded background would reintroduce the same erased boundary for any sprite
using them. Consequences: render.ts gains a background option (Wave 4);
critique.ts computes the colour (Wave 7); spec 4.4 and 6.8 amended.
